Review of the template concept

Keep

  • The category system. Six clear content types map almost exactly onto what the data says works.
  • Anton + gold on black. Reads at thumbnail size and feels like martial arts, not a gym.
  • Series numbering. "Bo Staff Basics 01, 02, 03" gives people a reason to follow.
  • Simple highlight icons. One line weight, one colour.

Fix before using

  • Use only real photos. The mockups show AI-generated versions of Kunal and an AI police scene. Followers will notice the face is off, and a fake police photo undercuts the one thing that makes him credible. Pull frames from his own reels.
  • Respect the grid crop. His profile grid shows 3:4, so the top and bottom 240px of a reel cover disappear. The maker shows this line; keep the badge and hook inside it.
  • Number = series episode. In the mockup grid, 01–12 is tile order. Only number series posts, otherwise the badge means nothing.
  • Don't brand the personal posts heavily. Personal content is his best performer because it feels real. Use a lighter cover or the photo alone for "Off the Mat" and family moments.
  • Cut text on covers. At grid size only the hook reads. Five-item list panels are fine on a carousel cover in the feed, but the list belongs on the slides.
  • Typos and clipping in the mockups: "FIFENESS" on Meet Kunal, "WRONG GRIP" clipped by the panel, a cut-off "REEL" label on template 06.

Cover rules

  1. One hook, 3–4 words. If it needs a fifth word, it goes in the supporting line.
  2. One accent per cover. Brush stroke or handwriting or icons, rarely all three.
  3. Action frame for skill, face for story. A mid-move frame for tutorials; eye contact for personal and self-defence.
  4. Gold for the word that matters. Usually the topic ("Self-Defence", "Basics 01"), never the filler.
  5. Check the grid preview before posting. If three gold-heavy tiles sit in a row, swap in a personal one.
  6. Students on camera need permission, and a parent's consent for anyone under 18.
